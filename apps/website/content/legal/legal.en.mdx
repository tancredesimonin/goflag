## The CLI collects nothing

`@goflag/cli` has no telemetry, no analytics, no crash reporting and no update
check. It sends no data to me or to anyone else. The only network traffic it
generates is HTTP requests to the site whose URL you passed on the command line,
and any redirects that site issues.

Reports are written to your disk or your terminal. Nothing is uploaded. There is
no account to create, so there is nothing to associate a run with.

You do not have to take my word for it: the source is public, and the network
layer is one directory.

## This website

**Analytics.** If analytics are enabled on this deployment, they run on
[Plausible](https://plausible.io), self-proxied through this domain. Plausible
sets no cookies, collects no personal data, and does not track visitors across
sites. It records page views, referrers, and coarse device and country
information, aggregated. There is no way for me to identify you from it.

**No cookies.** This site sets none. Your theme preference and nothing else is
kept in your browser's local storage, on your device, and is never sent anywhere.

**No third-party embeds.** No fonts loaded from a third party at runtime, no
tag manager, no chat widget, no advertising, no social buttons that phone home.

**Server logs.** The host records the usual request metadata — IP address, URL,
user agent, timestamp — for the operation and security of the server. They are
not used to build a profile and are not shared.

## Your rights

Since no personal data is collected, there is nothing to access, correct, port or
erase. If you believe otherwise, or you would like something clarified, open an
issue on the repository — it is the only channel, and it is public.

## No warranty, and what that means here

goflag reports findings about a website. It can be wrong — the
[Limits](/docs/limits) page lists the ways it has been wrong, with the cases that
were found and fixed. Nothing it prints is professional advice, and no verdict it
gives is a guarantee about how any search engine will treat your site. Decisions
you take on the strength of a report are yours.
